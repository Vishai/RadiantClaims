# Facebook Marketplace + Community Groups playbook

These are two channels distinct from your Page and from paid ads. Both can reach the RECA-eligible audience organically. Both require a fundamentally different posture than your Page content.

**The cardinal rule:** In community groups, you are a neighbor, not a business. The moment you sound like a business, you get removed, banned, or reported. Value-first or not at all.

---

## 1. Facebook Marketplace — service listing

Marketplace is primarily for buying/selling physical goods, but "Services" is a valid category. Brandon's service fits there.

```
(If "Legal" is not selectable due to your not-a-law-firm positioning, use "Services → Other" and lead with "claim preparation" wording)

The RECA program ends December 2027. Eligible claims include downwinder residents of Utah, Nevada, Arizona, New Mexico, and Idaho during the 1950s–'60s, as well as uranium miners, millers, and ore transporters pre-1986, and their surviving family members.

```

### Images to attach

Marketplace allows up to 10 images. Use:
1. `ad2-map-v2.png` (desk with southwest painting) — hero image
2. `ad1-hands-v1.png` (hands holding family photo)
3. `public/brand/facebook/cover-FINAL.png` (brand cover)
4. `ad3-notebook-v2.png` (notebook + mug)
5. `ad4-karen-v1.png` (Karen persona)

### Marketplace rules to know

- **No external links in the description.** Marketplace strips them. Anyone interested messages you.
- **Re-list every 7 days.** Marketplace deprioritizes stale listings. Delete and re-post weekly.
- **Respond within 1 hour** during business hours. Facebook surfaces "typically responds within X minutes" on every listing, and fast responders get algorithmic boost.
- **Don't use Marketplace for ad-style promotion.** The moment you start cross-posting to Marketplace from your Page ads, you're violating the spirit of the channel.

### Realistic expectations

Marketplace will probably generate **1–5 inquiries per week** at best. It's a cheap, low-effort channel — not a primary acquisition source. Treat it as free insurance.

## 2. Community groups — strategy first

### Which groups to target

You want groups where your audience already gathers **without you.** Search Facebook for these categories:

**Tier 1 — Perfect-fit (highest leverage, smallest audience)**
- "Downwinders" (capital D — there are several national and regional groups)
- "RECA Claimants" (if one exists)
- "Nevada Test Site Downwinders"
- "Four Corners Uranium Workers" / "Diné Uranium Workers"
- "Utah Nuclear Testing Families"
- Private/closed groups linked from `downwinderslearning.org`, `ccrj.org`, or the National Atomic Testing Museum Facebook

**Tier 2 — Adjacent audiences (medium leverage)**
- Regional memory groups: "I Grew Up in St. George", "Southern Utah Memories", "Old Las Vegas", "Cedar City History", "Grew Up in Gallup NM"
- County historical societies with active FB presence (Washington County UT, Iron County UT, San Juan County NM)
- Senior-living / retirement community groups in target ZIPs
- Church/ward community groups in affected areas (respect these — they're tight communities, high trust)
- Veterans' Facebook groups (overlap with atomic veterans population)

**Tier 3 — Broader but noisy (low per-post leverage, high reach)**
- Genealogy/family history groups (large audiences, some interested)
- Estate-planning help groups (Karen persona)
- Elder-caregiving groups (Karen persona)

**Don't target:**
- Legal advice groups (they'll flag you as unauthorized practice)
- Political groups (partisan noise; also see PLAYBOOK §11)
- Buy/sell/trade local groups (wrong context; posts get deleted)
- Competitor/industry groups where RECA-service operators lurk

### Group-admin outreach template

Always message the admin **before** your first post in any group. This is the #1 thing that separates durable group engagement from being banned in week 1.

```
Hi [admin name],

I run RadiantClaims (radiantclaims.com) — a small document-prep service helping downwinder families file for federal RECA compensation before the 2027 deadline. I'm not a law firm; we handle the forms and document checklists that the DOJ requires.

I'd love to contribute to the group, but I wanted to ask first:

1. Are posts from service providers allowed at all in [Group Name]?
2. If so, under what format? Do you have a preferred way for providers to introduce themselves?
3. Are there weekly threads or themes I should be aware of so I don't step on them?

I've drafted a few posts that are purely educational — the kind of thing I'd post whether or not RadiantClaims existed, answering questions your members might have about RECA eligibility, document requirements, and the DOJ process. Happy to share them in advance if that's useful.

Thanks for running the group. These communities matter and I don't want to be another drive-by operator.

— Brandon Armstrong
[Your email]
[Your personal FB profile — not the Page]
```

**Do this from your personal profile, not the Page.** Admins trust people over brands.

## 3. Community group post templates

Each of these is designed to be **value-first, link-optional**. No post should include a URL in the body. If someone asks in the comments, you reply with the link there.

### Template A — "Ask the community" (highest engagement, zero promotional)

Use in: Downwinder groups, regional memory groups, county history groups.

```
A question for anyone who grew up in [County/Town]:

Do you remember your parents or grandparents talking about the tests? Not the news coverage — the private conversations, the things they said when the kids were supposed to be asleep.

I'm building a document-prep service for RECA claims and I keep hearing the same thing from families: "My dad didn't like to talk about it." "My mom always washed the laundry twice after hanging it outside." "We used to play in the dust."

What stuck with you? No need to share anything you're not comfortable with. Just trying to understand what those years looked like through the eyes of the families who were there.

(I won't quote anyone. If you'd rather share privately, my DMs are open.)
```

**Why this works:** Asks, doesn't sell. Signals you understand the lived experience. High reply rate; every reply is a conversation that might become a customer relationship later.

**Rules:**
- Never include a link in this post
- If someone asks "what do you do?" in comments, respond with a one-sentence answer + link in the same comment
- Never DM participants to pitch them — that's how you get banned

---

### Template B — "Historical anniversary" (Pillar 2 crossover, works in history groups)

Use in: Regional memory groups, "Grew Up In…" groups, history-oriented county groups.

```
On this day in 1953, the federal government detonated "Simon" — an 11-kiloton atomic bomb — at the Nevada Test Site. The fallout drifted east across southern Nevada and into Utah, reaching Cedar City and St. George by evening.

Most people in those towns weren't told anything was wrong. Newspapers called the dust "harmless." Schoolchildren were sent to recess.

Seventy-three years later, the Radiation Exposure Compensation Act (RECA) still serves the families affected by tests like that one. It covers residents, their spouses and children, and specific categories of workers.

If your family was in southern Utah, Nevada, Arizona, New Mexico, or Idaho during those years, their story is part of this history.

(Not selling anything — just wanted to mark the date.)
```

**Why this works:** Dates create conversation. History-oriented groups welcome dated content. The parenthetical "not selling" line preempts the reflex scroll-past.

---

### Template C — "Document explainer" (Pillar 3 crossover, works in caregiver groups)

Use in: Elder-caregiving groups, estate-planning help groups, genealogy groups (Karen persona audience).

```
If you're helping an older parent navigate any kind of federal claim — RECA, VA, Social Security survivor benefits — one document keeps coming up: a certified death certificate with a raised state seal.

A few things worth knowing:

- Most states charge $15–$25 per copy
- Request THREE, not one. You'll need copies for multiple agencies
- Some states (UT, NV, NM) have online fast-track options; AZ and ID are still mail-in
- A funeral home death certificate ≠ a state-certified death certificate — the agencies want the state version

This is one of those small-but-painful things nobody tells you until you're already in the middle of it. Hope it helps someone.

(If you're navigating RECA specifically — the federal RECA deadline is December 2027 — and you'd like the state-specific request letter templates, I've pulled them together. Happy to share if useful.)
```

**Why this works:** Genuinely useful even if the reader never becomes a customer. The last paragraph is soft-offer — they can opt in via DM or ignore.

### Template D — "I lost someone too" (Founder story, adapted for community warmth)

Use in: Downwinder groups, grief-support groups (use carefully — ask admin first), veteran-family groups.

```
I'm new to this group. Hoping I don't overstep by posting.

I lost my mom to cancer in 2019. She was one of thousands of downwinders whose lives were shaped by the testing years. My dad has two eligible RECA claims of his own, and watching him try to figure out the DOJ paperwork last year is why I started RadiantClaims.

I'm not here to sell anyone anything. A bunch of you have probably navigated this already. I mostly wanted to say — if any of you are in the thick of paperwork and need someone who has been through it to bounce questions off, my messages are open. Free, no strings.

Thanks for letting me join the group.
```

**Why this works:** Honest, personal, no call-to-action. You're a person introducing yourself, not a business pitching. This is high-risk/high-reward: do it only in groups where introductions are welcome.

**Only post this once per group, ever.** Repeating it in the same group looks performative.

### Template E — "State-specific deadline nudge" (medium-promotional, use sparingly)

Use in: Regional memory groups, county history groups, "Grew Up In [State]" groups.

```
Quick public-service note for folks with family ties to southern Utah (or Nevada / Arizona / New Mexico / Idaho):

The federal Radiation Exposure Compensation Act — RECA — has a hard deadline of **December 2027**. After that, no new claims are accepted. It covers residents from the testing years, uranium workers, their spouses, children, and in some cases grandchildren.

If you've been putting it off because the paperwork is daunting — you're not alone. The DOJ process isn't designed for regular families. There's a lot of help out there now (including free resources from legal aid clinics and community orgs), so if you've been stuck, it's a good time to ask.

Happy to point anyone toward the right resource — DM me.
```

**Why this works:** Frames itself as community service, names the deadline without pressure, opens the door for DM-based conversations without spamming the feed with links.

**Rules:**
- Only post once per group per quarter at most
- Always offer to point at resources, not products
- Never post this in the same week as another of your posts in the same group

### Template F — "I saw a question you asked" (responsive, not posted)

When you spot a question in a group that RECA knowledge can answer, reply as a person — not as your Page.

Example question in a group: "Did anyone else's dad work at the Nevada Test Site in the late 50s? I'm trying to find out if he was eligible for something."

Your reply:
```
If he worked onsite between 1951 and 1962, he likely qualifies under Section 4 of RECA (Onsite Participant). It's a separate track from the downwinder residents, and the eligibility rules are different — you can file for his benefit even if he's passed.

Biggest document hurdle is usually proof of presence (employer records, pay stubs, or a service record if he was DoD). The DOJ's own guidance is pretty dense but there's a plain-English breakdown in the RECA section of their website.

Happy to DM if you want to talk through the specific paperwork.
```

**Why this works:** You answered the question usefully, without a link, without a pitch. The DM offer is natural, not pushy. This is the single highest-converting pattern in community groups — people remember who helped them.

## 4. Cadence discipline

Across all community groups combined, max:
- **1 "ask the community" post per group per month**
- **1 educational post per group per month**
- **1 historical/anniversary post per group per quarter**
- **1 founder-introduction post per group, ever**
- **Unlimited helpful replies to others' questions** — this is your highest-leverage channel

Total posting load: 3–5 posts per week across your 10–15 target groups. Plus 5–15 helpful replies per week.

## 5. Response playbook (when people DM you)

When someone DMs from a group post:

**Don't:** Send a pitch. Send a link list. Ask for their email right away.

**Do:** Ask them one diagnostic question first.

Examples:
- "Thanks for reaching out. Before I point you at anything, can I ask — are you looking for yourself, or for a parent/grandparent who's passed?"
- "Happy to help. Quick question first: which state did your family live in during the testing years?"

Their answer tells you which persona you're talking to. Then you can point at the right resource (your eligibility check, our documents page, or — if they're complex — a referral to your Legal Rep partner).

**Goal of first DM:** A useful answer, not a conversion. They'll come back.

## 6. What not to do

1. **Don't cross-post Page posts to groups.** They read as ads. Write group-native content.
2. **Don't tag the RadiantClaims Page** in group posts unless the admin has explicitly welcomed that.
3. **Don't DM group members who haven't messaged you first.** Cold-DM is the fastest route to a ban.
4. **Don't post the same thing in multiple groups within the same week.** Facebook detects cross-group spam.
5. **Don't argue with people in comments.** If someone pushes back, stay neutral ("fair point, appreciate the perspective") or disengage.
6. **Don't post about competitors.** Ever. Even if someone else is doing something shady, silence is your response.
7. **Don't post political content** in these groups. This includes anything about current legislation, politicians, or party-adjacent framing. Stay on the program, off the politics.
