```
Q: What if my mom or dad passed away before I heard about RECA?

You can still file on their behalf. The program explicitly allows surviving spouses, children, parents, and grandchildren to file for a deceased loved one's compensation.

The rule: A RECA claim by a survivor pays out the same $50,000–$100,000 that the original claimant would have received.

Real example: If your father lived in southern Utah between 1951 and 1962 and later developed a covered illness — even if he passed years ago — you can still file. You'll need his death certificate, proof of residency, and medical records.

If you're not sure whether this applies to your family, our free 2-minute eligibility check will walk you through it.
```

### Post 8 — 12:00pm MT — Document checklist
**Pillar 3 (Education).** First document-specific post. Pick Utah (largest eligible population).

```
Document you'll need: Certified death certificate

What the DOJ requires: An official, state-issued death certificate with the raised seal. Photocopies and unofficial versions are not accepted.

Where to get it (Utah): Utah Vital Records Office in Salt Lake City, or online through VitalChek. You'll need the deceased's full name, date of death, place of death, and your relationship to them.

How long it takes: 7–14 business days by mail. Same-day if you go in person.

Cost: $22 per certified copy in Utah.

Pro tip: Request three certified copies, not one. One for your RECA file, one for the DOJ, one backup. You'll also likely need copies for the hospital records request and any estate work.

We include pre-filled request letters for death certificates in all five RECA-eligible states in our guided prep flow.

#RECA #Downwinders #Utah
```
